**Hardware.** Every training, evaluation and latency run reported here, v2.6 to v2.13, was executed locally on a single Apple Silicon M1 laptop with **8 GB of unified memory** (PyTorch MPS for the GPU; CPU and GPU share the 8 GB). (For v2.13 the unit tests, browser verification and documentation build were also run on a Windows 11 PC, CPU only; no Windows timing is reported as a latency figure.) The memory limit shaped the design: model size, batch size, one heavy process at a time, and the latency figures, which come from a fanless laptop that throttles under sustained load (v2.10 §4.12.3).

limit δ_lim, which is still a development placeholder awaiting the consulting engineer's reference ratings (H3).

# Part A — Synthesis

## A.1 System summary (final state, v2.13)

A street photo of a pole goes through the following stages.

1. **Ingestion** (v2.10.1). Every readable image is accepted and normalised to 8-bit RGB (EXIF rotation,
   16-bit, alpha, CMYK, palette) and capped at 2048 px. GPS is stripped at the JPEG-segment level, so the
   analysed pixels are the uploaded pixels. Tiny or blank photos are accepted and graded Retake.
2. **Pre-processing** (Algorithm 1). A 1024 px letterbox for segmentation. Measured, triggered enhancement:
   edge-preserving denoise, CLAHE for low contrast or glare (not for darkness, v2.8), and blur-adaptive unsharp
   masking. A separate 640 px grey path for the VNI, never enhanced (Section 3.6.2).
3. **Segmentation.**
   - **Vegetation** comes from YOLO11s-seg at 1024.
   - **Wires** come from **WireNet**, a full-resolution U-Net (ResNet-34, ImageNet-pretrained) trained with
     cross-entropy + Dice + clDice. It uses flip test-time augmentation (v2.8) and, for dark photos,
     dual-polarity inference (v2.11).
   - Optional (v2.12, not shipped): a three-model probability fusion of WireNet-R34 with MiT-B1 and ConvNeXt-Tiny
     U-Nets, selected by naming `models/v212_ensemble.json` in `segmentation.wirenet_weights`. More accurate on
     validation, but about 4.7 s per photo on the M1.
   - **Ground gate** (v2.13): a pretrained ADE20K scene parser (UperNet-ConvNeXt-T, MIT licence, 384 px,
     0.26 s on the M1) labels road, sidewalk, floor and other ground. A wire span lying ≥ 50% on ground is a kerb,
     manhole or pavement edge: it is returned in `suppressed_spans` with its reason and never scored. The parser is
     skipped when no span reaches the lower half of the frame.
   - YOLO-seg masks were abandoned for wires because their 1/4-resolution prototype grid caps a 2 px wire at a
     median IoU of 0.40 (v2.7 §4.3.1).
4. **Span assembly** (v2.7, v2.8): Zhang–Suen skeleton, junction splitting with a 5 px cluster radius,
   collinear re-joining at 35°, collinear gap bridging (70 px, 25°), minimum stitched length 90 px.
5. **Chapter III mathematics, unchanged**:
   - Zhang–Suen centreline with spur pruning (Algorithm 4);
   - Levenberg–Marquardt catenary fit y = a·cosh((x − b)/a) + c (Eq. 6);
   - sag ratio δ/L (Eq. 8); allowable sag δ_lim (Eq. 9);
   - CRS = min(10, 10·(δ/L)/δ_lim·w) (Eq. 10), banded Low < 3 ≤ Medium ≤ 6 < High (Table 1.2);
   - VNI from a Hann-windowed 2-D FFT, MSPI ranking.
   CRS is computed only for chords ≥ 100 px, the measurement-precision gate σ/L ≤ ¼·δ_lim (v2.8 §4.6.2).
6. **Interpretation layer**, which reads the CRS and changes none of it:
   - **The pole verdict** AT RISK – HIGH / MODERATE / NOT AT RISK / INCONCLUSIVE – RETAKE PHOTO. A sag band
     counts only when more spans are in it than the measured per-span test–retest false-alarm rate explains (a
     one-sided binomial test, p0 = 0.050 / 0.090, α = 0.05; v2.9). Vegetation contact rules are absolute (v2.8).
   - **The reliability grade** A / B / C / Retake, from R = 1 − (U + D)/2 (v2.8), with a plain-language cause
     (v2.9) and a photometric glare gate (v2.10).
7. **Outputs.**
   - The field screener (v2.11, redesigned in v2.13): a hero verdict card (HAZARD DETECTED / REVIEW REQUIRED /
     NOT AT RISK / RETAKE PHOTO, worst CRS, measured vs total spans, one next action), the photo with Original /
     Detected spans / Full wire mask views and an overlay opacity slider, every span with its reason and the action
     that would let it be measured (v2.13), and a collapsed diagnostics drawer.
   - The admin command centre.
   - A PDF report with the three-panel diagnostic figure.
   - A batch street-audit CLI with a LabelMe review queue.

## A.2 Data (v2.5, v2.7 §4.1)

- **Dataset:** Wireframes-Prelabelled-Datasets, a span-level polygon export; 145 photos, split 102 train /
  22 validation / 21 test.
- **Classes:** Vegetation, abandoned-wire, power-distribution-lines, telecom-cable.
- **External sources:** none passed the five-gate screen (polygon masks, ground-level viewpoint, span-level
  labels, allowed licence, mappable classes). The search was done in v2.7 and repeated in v2.11, which added
  WireSegHR (no licence), MPCD (aerial) and railway OCS (catenary).
- **Validation and test use:** validation decided every rule and constant. Test use is accounted for in A.6.

**A recurring finding.** Pixel accuracy and span accuracy have disagreed four times: YOLO masks (v2.7), the
motion router (v2.9), hysteresis (v2.11) and the single v2.12 backbones (span mAP50 up, telecom pixel F1 down). The
three-model fusion is the first change to raise both. The screener needs continuous, separated spans, so span-level
validation decides, with pixel F1 as a guard.

## A.6 Methodological integrity: how the test split was used

- **No rule, threshold or constant was chosen on test.** Every choice was made on the 22 validation photos.
- **The test split was evaluated more than once in v2.9.** A coupling bug (service drops counted as failed wires in
  the blur fallback) was found because a locked run differed from v2.8. p0 was then re-measured on validation over
  all spans. Every superseded run is kept in `results/superseded/` (v2.9 §4.11.6).
- **v2.10:** the gate was chosen on validation, and the test glare evaluation was run once. The motivating failure
  turned out to be verdict instability, not glare dropout. The exposure-only alternative that would have caught it
  was **not** adopted, because choosing it after a test failure would leak test information (v2.10 §4.12.4).
- **v2.11:** the test run was interrupted once and completed by a second process, which gave identical duplicate
  rows; these were removed (v2.11 §4.13.5).
- **v2.12:** backbones and fusion weights were selected on validation only (15 grid points with the mirrored pass,
  15 without). The gain was checked with a paired bootstrap (94.4% of resamples, interval includes zero), and the
  winner's score is called optimistic. The fusion was rejected on latency, so the frozen v2.12 equals v2.11. The test
  split was run once on that frozen pipeline; no fusion was ever run on test.
- **v2.13:** every pre-processing, bridging and span-rule candidate, and the gate threshold, were decided on
  validation. The gate's benefit was shown on unlabelled live photos, which are not part of any split. The test
  split is to be run once, after freezing (**[PENDING QUIET M1 RUN]**).

## A.7 Open problems, in order of expected impact

1. **δ_lim calibration and reference ratings (H3).** Every sag band depends on the placeholder δ_lim = 0.040.
2. **Verdict instability.** The pole verdict changes in 41% of mildly degraded validation photos whose wires survive
   (v2.10 §4.12.4). A steadier per-span sag measurement is needed.
3. **Perspective.** Spans that recede in depth read high 3.3× more often (v2.12). Image-space sag is
   perspective-sensitive, and a metric-depth correction needs ground truth.
4. **Telecom recall.** Telecom recall at the operating threshold is 0.05 on test (0.025 at night).
5. **Real night data.** Night gains are measured on synthetic night photos, and YOLO vegetation is unvalidated at
   night.
6. **Abandoned wire.** 27 labelled instances are not enough to learn the class.
7. **Street-level false wires.** v2.13 removes traces on the road and pavement, but fence and railing traces in
   front of facades remain (no safe cue without retraining), and the gate's effect has no labelled street-level
   measurement yet.
8. **Glare and motion blur** remain the hardest capture conditions. The reliability grade and the glare gate turn
   them into "retake", not into results.
9. **Compute.** The more accurate fusion (v2.12) is blocked only by latency on an 8 GB M1. On faster hardware it is
   the first configuration to test, after re-checking p0 and the grade on validation and running test once.
