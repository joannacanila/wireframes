# WIREFRAME help for residents

## What my result means {#result}
Your result names what the photo showed for the pole. "Reference threshold exceeded" means a power line dips more than 3% of the straight line between its ends in the photo, so it should be checked by a qualified person. "No threshold exceedance detected" means every span that could be measured is within its reference. "Incomplete assessment" means nothing measured exceeds its reference but some spans could not be measured. "Measurement unavailable" means no span could be measured from this photo.
## What estimated sag means {#sag}
Estimated relative sag is how far a wire dips in the middle, compared with the straight line between its ends in the photo. For example, 4.9% means the dip is about 5 parts in 100 of that length. The estimated measurement range beside it shows how much the estimate could vary. It is an estimate from one photo, not a measurement in metres, and it cannot tell whether a wire is safe.
## The comparison point {#comparison}
For power lines, the system uses a 3% reference from ERB Resolution No. 95-21 (1995). Exceeding it in a photo is a screening result, not proof that a law is broken or that the wire is dangerous: it means the span is worth an inspection by a qualified person. Telecom and fiber cables are compared with typical ranges for their type instead (2% to 5%). Wires whose type the photo does not show clearly are listed but not classified.
## Is the wire dangerous or safe {#danger}
The system cannot tell whether a wire is dangerous or safe. It only looks at one photo. Keep a safe distance, treat every wire as live, and report your concern. If a wire is down, sparking or burning, or someone is hurt, call 911 right away.

## Staying safe {#safety}
- Keep a safe distance from the pole and the wires.
- Treat every wire as live. Do not touch, climb toward or try to move any wire.
- Do not try to inspect or fix it yourself.
- If a wire is down, sparking or burning, or someone is hurt, call 911 first.

## Who to contact {#contacts}
The contacts on your result page are suggestions based on where the photo was taken. Your barangay hall can help with any concern about poles and wires. The electric utility handles power lines; telecom providers handle their cables. The system does not know who owns a wire, so a listed company is a suggested place to report, not a confirmed owner. Use the Call or Website buttons on your result page. If a number says "confirm on the official site", check the company's own website first.

## Reporting the concern {#report}
Tell the organization where the pole is, what you noticed, and share your report. You can download the report as a PDF or share a link to your result. The report includes your photo, the wire to look at and the contacts.

## Downloading or sharing the report {#share}
Use "Download report" on your result page to save a PDF. Use "Share" to send a link to your result by message or email. Anyone with the link can open the result, so share it only with people who need it.

## Telecom and fiber cables {#telecom}
Telecom and fiber cables (internet, phone and cable TV lines) are compared with general ranges, not with the power-line point. Below about 2% is the typical range. Between about 2% and 5% depends on the cable design and how it was installed. Above about 5% is beyond the typical ranges and worth reporting to the provider or your barangay. These ranges come from industry guidance, not from a rule for one particular cable.

## Wires the system could not check {#not-checked}
Some wires cannot be measured from one photo, for example when they cross other wires, run off the edge of the photo, or blend into the background. That does not mean they are fine. A new photo that shows both poles and the whole wire against open sky may help.

## Taking a better photo {#photo}
Stand where you can see both poles and the whole wire between them, with open sky behind it. Keep the phone level and steady, in daylight. Do not get close to the wires to take the photo.

## What happens to my photo {#privacy}
Your photo and result are kept so they can be shared and reviewed. Anyone with the link to your result can open it.

## What this assistant can do {#about}
I can explain your result, what estimated sag means, how to stay safe, who to contact and how to download or share your report. I cannot look up other records, contact anyone for you, send inspectors, or change your result.
